Task 2. Keep 5 dB and $b=1.5$, and raise the samples from 2,000 to 8,000. Predict the new RMS error, and whether the sample ratio changes.

Four times as many samples halve the RMS error, from 0.041 to 0.021. The sample ratio stays at about 26, because it compares the two settings at the same number of samples.
