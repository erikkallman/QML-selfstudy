In quantum terms, the device is the channel $\Lambda(\rho)=\mathbb{E}_\alpha\left[D(\alpha)\rho D(\alpha)^\dagger\right]$.
From $D(\alpha)^\dagger aD(\alpha)=a+\alpha$ ("Background") it follows that
$D(\alpha)^\dagger D(\beta)D(\alpha)=e^{\alpha^*\beta-\beta^*\alpha}D(\beta)$ for every $\beta$.
The mean over $\alpha$ gives
$$\Lambda^\dagger\big(D(\beta)\big)=\lambda(\beta)\,D(\beta),$$
where $\Lambda^\dagger$ is the channel in the Heisenberg picture.
So each $D(\beta)$ is an eigenoperator of the channel, and $\lambda(\beta)$ is its eigenvalue. This is the reason for the symbol $\lambda$.
The operators $D(\beta)$ span all operators on the mode, so these eigenvalues determine the channel.
A Pauli channel on qubits has the same structure: the Pauli operators are its eigenoperators.
